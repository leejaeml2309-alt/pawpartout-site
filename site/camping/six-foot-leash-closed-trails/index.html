Six-Foot Leash, Closed Trails

Published 21 November 2026

A campsite can accept a dog and a trail beside it can still be closed to pets. The difference is written on official park pages, not in a packing list.

This note sets out National Park Service and Texas Parks & Wildlife Department rules as they appeared on those official pages on 21 November 2026. It is not a list of campgrounds, and it does not describe a trip we have already taken. Park rules vary. The page for the park you will actually visit has the last word.

National Park Service: start with the park’s own page

The National Park Service says that many national parks allow visits with a pet, but that different parks may have different rules, and that you should check the park website before you travel. NPS, Visit Parks — Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

A separate NPS subject page states that most national parks allow pets in developed areas, and that you should learn the limitations that apply to you and your pet. NPS, Things To Do With Your Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

NPS also publishes the B.A.R.K. principles used in the B.A.R.K. Ranger program: bag your pet’s waste; always leash your pet; respect wildlife; and know where you can go. That page does not itself state a leash length. NPS, Be a B.A.R.K. Ranger Verified 21 Nov 2026

The six-foot leash is in the regulation

The leash length used across the National Park System sits in the Code of Federal Regulations, not on the B.A.R.K. subject page. Under 36 CFR 2.15, it is prohibited to fail to crate, cage, restrain on a leash which shall not exceed six feet in length, or otherwise physically confine a pet at all times. 36 CFR 2.15 (eCFR) Verified 21 Nov 2026

The same section prohibits possessing a pet in a public building, a public transportation vehicle, a location designated as a swimming beach, or any structure or area closed to pets by the superintendent. That subparagraph does not apply to guide dogs accompanying visually impaired persons or hearing-ear dogs accompanying hearing-impaired persons. It is also prohibited to leave a pet unattended and tied to an object, except in designated areas or under conditions the superintendent may establish, and to fail to comply with pet excrement disposal conditions the superintendent may establish. 36 CFR 2.15 (eCFR) Verified 21 Nov 2026

Texas State Parks: a six-foot leash, and several closed doors

Texas Parks & Wildlife Department’s state parks FAQ says you can bring pets to almost all state parks, with these rules among others: the pet must be on a leash no longer than six feet, or in a car or a crate, at all times; you may not leave the pet unattended in the park, in a vehicle, or at your campsite; pets are not allowed in any state park buildings, including motels, cabins, screened shelters, group facilities and restrooms; you must pick up the pet’s waste and put it in the trash; pets are not allowed in the water or on the land around a designated swim area; and the pet must have a current rabies vaccination, with proof you have with you. The FAQ says some of these rules do not apply to service dogs helping a person with a disability. TPWD, State Parks FAQs — Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

The same FAQ lists parks with additional pet restrictions, including Big Bend Ranch State Park and Enchanted Rock State Natural Area. TPWD, State Parks FAQs — Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

Stricter maps: Big Bend Ranch and Enchanted Rock

At Big Bend Ranch State Park, TPWD restates the department-wide rules (a leash no longer than six feet; no dogs in buildings; pick up feces immediately; never leave the dog unattended). It then adds park-specific limits: dogs are only allowed within a quarter of a mile from campsites or designated roads, and only on the Closed Canyon Trail and the Hoodoos Trail on FM 170. Dogs are not allowed on any other hiking trails, in the backcountry, or more than a quarter of a mile from campsites or designated roads. The page notes that, under the Americans with Disabilities Act, these policies do not apply to persons relying on service dogs. TPWD, Policies on Dogs at Big Bend Ranch State Park Verified 21 Nov 2026

At Enchanted Rock State Natural Area, TPWD states that pets are only allowed in the designated day-use picnic areas, the campgrounds, and on the Loop Trail, and are not allowed on any other trail. Pets must not be left unattended, including in vehicles and at campsites, and must be kept on a leash no longer than six feet at all times. TPWD, Enchanted Rock State Natural Area Verified 21 Nov 2026

The pattern is the one this journal will keep returning to: the campground may be open to the dog, and the next trail may not. Read the park’s own page before you pack the leash.
